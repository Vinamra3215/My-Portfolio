Multi-Regime Quantitative Trading on Anonymized Time-Series

How we designed a systematic trading framework using volatility regime detection, adaptive indicators, and priority-based strategy selection to achieve 32% annualized returns at Inter IIT Tech Meet 14.0.

Multi-Regime Quantitative Trading on Anonymized Time-Series

QUANTITATIVE FINANCE · Inter IIT Tech Meet 14.0 — Ebullient Securities Challenge

I. Introduction

The Ebullient Securities Challenge at Inter IIT Tech Meet 14.0 required teams to develop systematic quantitative trading strategies on mid-frequency, anonymized multi-feature time-series data. The dataset consisted of two instruments — EBX and EBY — with hundreds of masked features spanning price, volatility, volume, and technical bands.

The core challenge was building a profitable, risk-controlled strategy without knowing what any feature actually represented — pure statistical signal extraction from opaque data.

II. Architecture: Multi-Regime Framework

Our approach was built on a tiered pipeline:

  • Market Regime Detection → Classify each trading day by volatility regime
  • Strategy Selection → Dynamically pick the best strategy via a priority-based manager
  • Signal Generation → Execute trades with adaptive indicators and strict risk controls

Volatility Regime Classification

We computed 30-minute opening volatility (σ) and mean price (μ) for each day, then segmented days into regimes:

  • High Volatility (Strategy 1): σ > σ75 and μ > μthreshold → Short-first bias. Price spikes likely to be sold off.
  • Mid Volatility (Strategy 2): σ50 ≤ σ ≤ σ75 and μ < μthreshold → Long-first bias. Price dips likely to recover.

If neither condition is met, the system stays flat — no trade is the best trade when conditions don't favor us.

III. Core Strategies

1. Sigma-Triggered Alpha Reactor (STAR)

A three-phase sequential reversal strategy adapted to the volatility regime:

  • Gap-Fill Entry: Counter-trend position at the 30-minute mark when price is outside Open ± Threshold
  • Flip: On target hit, close initial position → cooldown → open high-conviction position in favored direction with trailing stop loss
  • Position Management: Trailing stops + reversal targets + end-of-day square-off

2. Volatility-Filtered KAMA Trend

Day segregation using ATR and STD thresholds. On filtered "tradable" days, applies the Kaufman Adaptive Moving Average (KAMA) slope to detect trend direction and generate long/short signals with take-profit barriers.

3. Hawkes Adaptive Trend Strategy

Captures large, sustained movements while avoiding chop:

  • Adaptive Trend: Price smoothed via Ehlers Super Smoother → AURA filter
  • Impulse Detection: Hawkes Process measures momentum burst intensity
  • Dynamic Volatility Walls: Adaptive support/resistance boundaries
  • Entry: Only when validated Hawkes impulse + volatility wall + trend slope alignment

4. SuperNova Crossover

Volatility-gated AURA-JMA crossover — only activates when both ATR and rolling standard deviation gates are met. Trades based on crossovers between a slow AURA and the Jurik Moving Average, with independent signal management where exits trigger delayed counter-entries.

IV. Dynamic Priority-Based Strategy Manager

We used a priority-based locking method with pre-assigned priorities. Each strategy's lock resets daily:

  • EBX: Denoised Trend Pattern → Volatility-Filtered KAMA → STAR
  • EBY: Denoised Trend Pattern → STAR → Volatility-Filtered KAMA → Money Heist

The selector cascades through strategies, ensuring the most reliable one is always selected for prevailing conditions.

VI. Key Takeaways

  • Custom Indicators: AURA, Ehlers Super Smoother, and Hawkes Process — designed specifically for the anonymized data's statistical properties
  • Regime Awareness: The system knows when not to trade. Flat positions during unfavorable regimes prevented most drawdown
  • Risk Management: Z-score-based position sizing and cascading controls automatically adjusted exposure based on market conditions
  • No Feature Engineering Bias: Since features were anonymized, all signals were derived purely from statistical properties — no domain assumptions leaked in
